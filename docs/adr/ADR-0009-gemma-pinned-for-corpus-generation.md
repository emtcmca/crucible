## Context

The attack corpus is hash-locked and frozen before the loop runs. That is what makes the
result pre-registered rather than reported. A pre-registration is only worth something if a
third party can check it, and checking it means regenerating the corpus and getting the same
hash.

A hosted model cannot support that. `gemini-3.7-flash` retires 45 days after a replacement
ships, with no announced date (`CONVENTIONS.md:227`). If the corpus ever needs regenerating
and the model moved underneath it, the corpus hash changes and the pre-registration is void.

## Decision

**Corpus generation runs on open-weights Gemma, pinned by version and seed**
(`CONVENTIONS.md:208`, `architecture-spec.md:1220`). `CAPABILITY_CARTOGRAPHER` runs on the
same footing (`architecture-spec.md:138`).

**Hosting: Cloud Run + NVIDIA L4, `min-instances=0`** — Eric's ruling, `CONVENTIONS.md` §3.3.
This supersedes an earlier line in that file that said *do NOT stand up a Cloud Run GPU*; the
danger is leaving an instance warm, not the GPU itself. A ~30-minute generation burst costs
**~$0.34**.

**The rationale, and it is the only one that may be written anywhere:** an open-weights model
pinned by version and seed is the only way corpus generation is reproducible by a third
party. On camera, one clause: *"the attack corpus is generated by an open-weights model
pinned by version and seed, because a corpus you can't regenerate is a corpus you can't
pre-register."*

Gemma also carries **+0.2 of the +0.6 additional-model bonus**, which is why it has a real
architectural home rather than a bolted-on one (`execution-spec.md:615`).

## The alternative that was rejected, and why

**The old rationale is itself the most important rejection.** *"Aligned frontier models refuse
to author red-team payloads at volume"* is **struck and must not be written anywhere,
including comments and this ADR** (`execution-spec.md:24`, `:372`, `CONVENTIONS.md:221`). True
or not, it reads as *"the model was chosen to route around safety refusals,"* in a contest
Google is judging. It was the single most quotable line against this project. The
corresponding demo artifact was struck with it: **do not capture or publish a refusal pair** —
it stages the struck framing as evidence (`execution-spec.md:382`).

**Hosting alternatives** (`CONVENTIONS.md:242-250`):

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Vertex AI Endpoint, self-deployed | Bills **per node-hour continuously**, idle or not — the same shape as the Vertex Vector Search trap |
| Vertex Model Garden managed API | **Fallback only.** Simpler, but you control neither the container nor the weights, which weakens the reproducibility claim |
| GKE | A cluster bills whether or not workloads run, and Kubernetes is a stated weak area |

## Consequences

- **The reproducibility check is the artifact, not a screenshot:** Gemma regenerates the
  corpus from the pinned version and seed and the hash matches, **twice, from a clean
  process** (`execution-spec.md:382`).
- **`min-instances=0` is the one rule.** L4 is $0.672/hr and GPUs are excluded from the free
  tier; `min-instances=1` costs **$193 over twelve days**, more than the entire cap, for a
  service used for half an hour. Verify after deploy by reading the annotation, not by
  trusting the deploy output (`CONVENTIONS.md:252-256`).
- Gemma is cut candidate 2 (`execution-spec.md:475`). Cutting it loses 0.2 of the ceiling, the
  second-model story, and **third-party reproducibility of the corpus** — which is the real
  cost and should be stated rather than glossed.

## What this does not decide

- Which model runs any other role. That roster is `CONVENTIONS.md` §3.1.
- The corpus contents or the D5 freeze procedure.
